A sharp Wigner–Husimi bound for phase-invariant light

Abstract

Gagatsos conjectured an upper bound on single-mode Wigner negativity in terms of the Husimi distribution. We prove the conjectured inequality for every photon-number-diagonal state, with no cutoff on photon number. The vacuum is the unique state in this class attaining finite equality. The proof bounds a Laguerre absolute integral by an exactly computable fourth moment and compares that moment with the Husimi integral by induction.

Arbitrary photon-number statistics

Let ρ=∑n≥0pn|n⟩⟨n|\rho=\sum_{n\ge0}p_n\lvert n\rangle\!\langle n|. Physical check.

For one photon, L1(u)=1−uL_1(u)=1-u. Splitting (1) at u=1u=1 gives A1=4e−1/2−1A_1=4e^{-1/2}-1, whereas B1=π/2B_1=\pi/2. The Wigner negative volume is 2e−1/2−1≈0.21312e^{-1/2}-1\approx0.2131 and the heterodyne upper bound is (π/2−1)/2≈0.2854(\pi/2-1)/2\approx0.2854. This one-photon value is known [2]. The theorem establishes the bound for every photon count and every probability distribution over counts. It leaves the general question for photon-number coherences open. Applying the same displacement to the whole state simply translates both integrals.

Edition note.

For a diagonal mixture, the probabilities appear linearly in the upper bound on the Wigner norm. The integral triangle inequality puts the same weighted sum of number-state Husimi bounds below the Husimi functional of the mixture. This step admits infinite photon-number support and uses the absence of off-diagonal terms in the number basis.
