The optimal check weight of an [[11,5,3]] binary stabilizer code is seven

Project initiated and directed by Aryan Singh.
oo research · The Open University (student)

26 November 2026

Abstract

How many data qubits must the largest stabilizer measurement touch when eleven physical qubits protect five logical qubits against an arbitrary single-qubit error? Wei et al. list the possible values as six through eight. Steane’s 1996 code already admits a weight-seven basis. Jing and Tu also settled the optimum using SAT certificates. We give an elementary proof of the same result. To rule out six, we count the syndromes of single-site Pauli errors. Minimum total incidence would force a distribution of syndrome weights incompatible with commutation. An adapted basis and a shortening argument cover degenerate codes. Both bounds concern arbitrary binary stabilizer codes, including non-CSS codes, with no geometric restriction.

An attaining code

Let WW be the smallest possible maximum Pauli weight of a generating set, minimized over the codes with the specified parameters. Deleting dependent generators never increases that maximum. A lower bound for every generating basis

Suppose six generators of weight at most six define a code of length eleven encoding five qubits with distance at least three. All Pauli statements below are modulo scalar phase. The syndrome of a Pauli is its vector of anticommutation bits against an independent stabilizer basis. Two errors have equal syndrome exactly when their product commutes with the stabilizer. Such a product of weight at most two must belong to the stabilizer by the distance hypothesis.

Remove fixed factors. A weight-one stabilizer fixes a single qubit. Two independent stabilizers on the same two-qubit support, in the absence of a weight-one stabilizer, fix a pure two-qubit factor. In the second case the local commuting Pauli space has dimension two and equals its own centralizer on that pair. The analogous statement holds in dimension one on a fixed qubit. Consequently the local restriction of any other check on the removed factor belongs to its local stabilizer. Strip the factor and take an independent subset of the restricted generators. This preserves five encoded qubits and the weight cap. A logical on the remainder lifts by identity to a logical of the same weight, so the distance remains at least three. Repeat until neither type of factor remains. Write nn for the remaining length and r=n−5r=n-5 for its stabilizer rank, with 0≤r≤60\le r\le6.

Account for repeated syndromes. None of the 3n3n single-site errors has zero syndrome, since a commuting error of weight one would fix a qubit. The three errors on each site have distinct syndromes for the same reason. Inside any class of equal syndromes, two distinct errors act on different sites and their product is a weight-two stabilizer. Two distinct repeated classes cannot share a site. Indeed, the different Pauli labels there anticommute. Taking a partner for each error gives two stabilizers that would anticommute unless both partners are on the same second site. In that event they are independent stabilizers supported on that pair, a factor already removed.

Let tt count all copies in excess of one per syndrome class. In each repeated class choose a root error and pair it with every other member. These tt weight-two products are independent: a leaf site occurs in only one selected product, and the classes have disjoint sites. Thus t≤rt\le r. Extend them to a stabilizer basis using checks from the weight-at-most-six generating set. A change of independent basis transforms syndrome vectors by an invertible binary matrix. It preserves which syndromes coincide and which are zero.

Let SS denote the sum of the Hamming weights of the 3n3n single-site syndromes in this adapted basis, counting repetitions. Each nonidentity letter of a generator anticommutes with two of the three local Paulis. Hence S=2∑a=1rwt⁡(ga)≤2(2t+6(r−t))=12r−8t.S=2\sum_{a=1}^r\operatorname{wt}(g_a)\ \le\ 2(2t+6(r-t))=12r-8t . There are 3n−t3n-t distinct nonzero rr-bit strings. Each of the tt extra copies contributes at least one to SS.

Exclude shortened cases. If r≤4r\le4, at least 3(r+5)−r=2r+153(r+5)-r=2r+15 distinct syndromes would have to fit in 2r−12^r-1 slots. This fails for every 0≤r≤40\le r\le4. If r=5r=5, the 30 cheapest distinct nonzero five-bit strings have total weight 5⋅16−5=755\cdot16-5=75 (omit the all-ones string). Removing tt of the largest weights can save at most 5t5t. Therefore S≥75−5t+t>60−8tS\ge75-5t+t>60-8t, another contradiction.

Force equality at length eleven. For r=6r=6, the 33 cheapest distinct nonzero strings have weights one (six times), two (fifteen times) and three (twelve times). Their total weight is 7272. Keeping 33−t33-t distinct strings costs at least 72−3t72-3t, so S≥72−2tS\ge72-2t. Together with S≤72−8tS\le72-8t this forces t=0t=0 and equality. Every candidate now has exactly the syndrome weight distribution just given.

Use commutation. For each pair of distinct basis checks a,ba,b, the integer ∑esa(e)sb(e)\sum_e s_a(e)s_b(e) is even, where ee runs over all single-site errors. On a site where both checks have the same nonidentity Pauli, two errors anticommute with both. Where they have different nonidentity Paulis, exactly one does. All other sites contribute zero. The parity is therefore the commutator bit of the two checks, which is zero. Summing over all pairs requires ∑e(wt⁡(s(e))2)\sum_e\binom{\operatorname{wt}(s(e))}{2} even. The forced distribution gives 6(12)+15(22)+12(32)=51.6\binom{1}{2}+15\binom{2}{2}+12\binom{3}{2}=51. This contradiction rules out maximum weight six or less. The displayed code attains seven, proving W=7W=7.▫\square

Comparison and verification

AI contributions and verification.

AI assistance was used to draft the explanation of the weight-seven optimum. A separate project AI session checked the commutation argument and replayed the upper witness by signed Pauli multiplication. The arithmetic check of 28 cases depends on the written shortening and syndrome arguments. Jing and Tu had already obtained the optimum by a SAT proof. Internal mathematical review.

Wei, Han, He, Li and Liu, Theory of low-weight quantum codes, Table 2 (v2, September 2026) report the interval [6,8][6,8]. The lower bound applies across all such codes. Reconstructing the code in Eq. (29) of Steane, Simple quantum error-correcting codes (1996) gives six independent weight-seven generators. It has 75 weight-three logical Pauli strings versus 79 here, so qubit permutations and single-qubit Clifford gates cannot relate the two. For comparison, the Grassl construction and two entries in the QEC Challenge archive have minimum basis weight eight, as checked by enumerating their stabilizer spans. Earlier work on weight-two clusters for the distinct [[11,3,3]][[11,3,3]] question includes Zhang’s analysis. Jing and Tu had already proved seven optimal with 37 certified unsatisfiable instances in September 2026. The counting argument above is an alternative proof.

Reproduction package: the upper-bound check enumerates all relevant Pauli strings, and the lower-bound check exhausts 28 arithmetic cases after the proved reduction. The note has not undergone external peer review. Check weight measures the support of a Pauli measurement.
