The refined BMV trace inequalities
for a matrix with two spectral values

GPT-6.2 Astra (high) (lead AI author of this edition)
Aryan Singh ⋅\cdot oo research ⋅\cdot The Open University (student)

26 November 2026

Abstract

Let A,BA,B be positive semidefinite matrices of any finite size. If AA has at most two distinct eigenvalues, the mean trace of all words with nn letters AA and mm letters BB lies between Tr⁡[(An/mB)m]\operatorname{Tr}[(A^{n/m}B)^m] and Tr⁡(AnBm)\operatorname{Tr}(A^nB^m) for all positive integers n,mn,m. This follows by grouping Hägele’s path expansion according to the number of visits to one eigenspace. The resulting weights are coefficients of an ordinary Bessis–Moussa–Villani polynomial and are therefore nonnegative. Together with a known three-eigenvalue counterexample, this gives the sharp spectral threshold for the upper bound. An appendix independently reproduces finite cases of a previously established three-eigenvalue lower bound.

The two-sided bound

Write 𝒜n,m(A,B)=(n+mm)−1∑WTr⁡W\mathcal A_{n,m}(A,B)=\binom{n+m}{m}^{-1}\sum_W\operatorname{Tr}W, where WW runs over all words with nn letters AA and mm letters BB. Hägele’s refinement of the BMV conjecture asks whether, for Hermitian H,KH,K, the value of 𝒜n,m(eH,eK)\mathcal A_{n,m}(e^H,e^K) lies between the quantum partition function Tr⁡enH+mK\operatorname{Tr}e^{nH+mK} and Tr⁡(enHemK)\operatorname{Tr}(e^{nH}e^{mK}) [1]. Cha and Lee disproved the upper inequality in general [4]. Here the spectral multiplicities and both word counts are arbitrary.

Theorem.

If A,B∈Md(ℂ)A,B\in M_d(\mathbb C) are positive semidefinite and AA has at most two distinct eigenvalues, then Tr⁡[(An/mB)m]≤𝒜n,m(A,B)≤Tr⁡(AnBm)(n,m≥1).(1)\begin{equation} \operatorname{Tr}[(A^{n/m}B)^m]\ \le\ \mathcal A_{n,m}(A,B)\ \le\ \operatorname{Tr}(A^nB^m) \qquad(n,m\ge1). \qquad (1) \end{equation} For A=eHA=e^H and B=eKB=e^K, the first term dominates Tr⁡enH+mK\operatorname{Tr}e^{nH+mK} by the Araki–Lieb–Thirring inequality and the Lie–Trotter formula [2, Lemma 2]. Interchanging the two factors gives the partition-function and upper bounds also when KK has at most two spectral values.

Proof.

Write A=aP+bQA=aP+bQ with P,QP,Q complementary orthogonal projections and a,b≥0a,b\ge0. A scalar AA gives equality. Let ℛ={r∈ℕ0m:∑iri=n}\mathcal R=\{r\in\mathbb N_0^m\,:\,\sum_i r_i=n\}, of size N=(n+m−1m−1)N=\binom{n+m-1}{m-1}. By cyclicity [2, Lemma 3], 𝒜n,m(A,B)=N−1∑r∈ℛTr⁡(Ar1BAr2B⋯ArmB).\mathcal A_{n,m}(A,B)=N^{-1}\sum_{r\in\mathcal R} \operatorname{Tr}(A^{r_1}B A^{r_2}B\cdots A^{r_m}B). For example, marking one of the mm occurrences of BB in each word counts each word mm times. Rotating the marked letter to the end leaves a weak composition and a rotation offset in {0,…,n+m−1}\{0,\ldots,n+m-1\}. The trace is independent of that offset.

For a sequence E=(E1,…,Em)∈{P,Q}mE=(E_1,\ldots,E_m)\in\{P,Q\}^m, set w(E)=Tr⁡(E1BE2B⋯EmB)w(E)=\operatorname{Tr}(E_1 B E_2 B\cdots E_m B). Let λi=a\lambda_i=a when Ei=PE_i=P and λi=b\lambda_i=b when Ei=QE_i=Q. The scalar multiplying w(E)w(E) in the average is κk=N−1∑r∈ℛ∏iλiri,k=#{i:Ei=Q}.\kappa_k=N^{-1}\sum_{r\in\mathcal R}\prod_i\lambda_i^{r_i}, \qquad k=\#\{i:E_i=Q\}. It depends only on kk because ℛ\mathcal R is invariant under coordinate permutations. With ck=∑E:#Q=kw(E)c_k=\sum_{E:\#Q=k}w(E) we therefore have 𝒜n,m(A,B)=∑kckκk\mathcal A_{n,m}(A,B)=\sum_k c_k\kappa_k. Terms with exponent zero equal one, including at a zero eigenvalue.

Positivity of the grouped weights.

Put U=B1/2PB1/2U=B^{1/2}PB^{1/2} and V=B1/2QB1/2V=B^{1/2}QB^{1/2}. Both are positive semidefinite, and cyclicity gives p(t)=Tr⁡[(U+tV)m]=Tr⁡[((P+tQ)B)m]=∑k=0mcktk.p(t)=\operatorname{Tr}[(U+tV)^m]=\operatorname{Tr}[((P+tQ)B)^m]=\sum_{k=0}^m c_k t^k. The polynomial form of the ordinary BMV theorem gives ck≥0c_k\ge0 [3]. This use of the theorem permits arbitrary ranks of PP and QQ and arbitrary complex off-diagonal blocks of BB.

Scalar bounds.

Every coordinate rir_i has average n/mn/m over ℛ\mathcal R. The arithmetic–geometric mean inequality over compositions gives the lower bound below. For the upper bound, apply the weighted arithmetic–geometric mean inequality to ∏i(λin)ri/n\prod_i(\lambda_i^n)^{r_i/n} for each composition and average: (am−kbk)n/m≤κk≤(m−k)an+kbnm.(a^{m-k}b^k)^{n/m}\ \le\ \kappa_k\ \le\ \frac{(m-k)a^n+kb^n}{m}. Continuity extends the lower bound from positive eigenvalues to zero. After multiplication by ckc_k and summation, expansion of (An/mB)m(A^{n/m}B)^m gives the left side of (1). For the upper side, p(1)=Tr⁡Bmp(1)=\operatorname{Tr}B^m and p′(1)=mTr⁡[V(U+V)m−1]=mTr⁡(QBm)p'(1)=m\operatorname{Tr}[V(U+V)^{m-1}]=m\operatorname{Tr}(QB^m). Therefore ∑kck(m−k)an+kbnm=anTr⁡(PBm)+bnTr⁡(QBm)=Tr⁡(AnBm).▫\sum_k c_k\frac{(m-k)a^n+kb^n}{m} =a^n\operatorname{Tr}(PB^m)+b^n\operatorname{Tr}(QB^m)=\operatorname{Tr}(A^nB^m).\quad\square

An elementary check through five letters BB.

In the splitting P⊕QP\oplus Q, let BB have diagonal blocks X,YX,Y and upper-right block ZZ. With m≤5m\le5, a cyclic sequence of labels switches blocks zero, two or four times. With zero switches its weight is Tr⁡Xm\operatorname{Tr}X^m or Tr⁡Ym\operatorname{Tr}Y^m. Two switches give Tr⁡(XiZYjZ*)\operatorname{Tr}(X^i ZY^j Z^*) for some i,j≥0i,j\ge0. With four switches there is at most one diagonal step, giving Tr⁡[(ZZ*)2]\operatorname{Tr}[(ZZ^*)^2], Tr⁡[X(ZZ*)2]\operatorname{Tr}[X(ZZ^*)^2], or Tr⁡[Y(Z*Z)2]\operatorname{Tr}[Y(Z^*Z)^2], up to cyclic rotation. These traces are nonnegative because X,YX,Y are positive semidefinite and the trace of a product of two positive semidefinite matrices is nonnegative. Thus in these low degrees one obtains the required weight signs directly. If one eigenspace is one-dimensional, one can diagonalize the other diagonal block and adjust basis phases to make all entries nonnegative. The general theorem allows both eigenspaces to have arbitrary rank.

Scope, sharpness and an independent finite reproduction

The theorem is a direct corollary of the composition identity and ordinary BMV coefficient positivity. Priority for this particular deduction has not been established. Since 𝒜n,m(A,B)=𝒜m,n(B,A)\mathcal A_{n,m}(A,B)=\mathcal A_{m,n}(B,A), a violation of the upper inequality requires at least three spectral values in each factor. Cha and Lee’s example [4] attains that threshold: take A=1⊕xCA=1\oplus xC, B=xC⊕1B=xC\oplus1, C=(1−1−11)C=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&-1\\-1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right) and x=10−3x=10^{-3}. Both spectra equal {0,2x,1}\{0,2x,1\}. At n=m=5n=m=5 their example has 𝒜5,5(A,B)−Tr⁡(A5B5)>0\mathcal A_{5,5}(A,B)-\operatorname{Tr}(A^5B^5)>0 [4, Proposition 6]. Adding εI\varepsilon I to both matrices preserves the strict polynomial gap for sufficiently small ε>0\varepsilon>0. Both then have three distinct positive eigenvalues and Hermitian logarithms. Thus the threshold also holds for the original exponential inputs. The lower bound for general spectra remains open.

The lower comparison at m=4m=4 is covered by an existing three-value containment theorem. At arbitrary word counts, the lower comparison for A=tPA=tP follows from the spectral-pinching inequality, and a one-dimensional eigenspace permits the nonnegative-entry basis of [2, Proposition 1]. The priority audit found no previous general deduction for two positive levels of unrestricted multiplicities and all m≥5m\ge5.

Edition note.

Before applying either scalar inequality, group paths by how often they visit the second eigenspace. The ordinary BMV theorem gives the sign of each group total even when individual path traces have no known sign. The appendix uses three-eigenvalue computations to reproduce cases of an existing lower bound.

Appendix.

A public Lean proof [5] covers all positive integers nn with m=3m=3 when AA has at most three distinct eigenvalues, in any finite dimension. The following direct computation reproduces the subfamily d=3d=3, 11≤n≤3611\le n\le36 using Hägele’s sufficient criterion [2, Lemmas 5–6].
