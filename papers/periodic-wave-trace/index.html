Can a spectral band turn around inside its allowed range?

With a real periodic potential the slope cannot vanish there. An imaginary cosine potential can produce a stationary point inside the band. The answer depends on an assumption left unstated in the question.

The catalogue leaves the potential’s range unstated. Lean checks the real matrix inequality; the full argument treats both cases.

When a periodic wave’s spectral trace can turn

For a real, continuous, one-periodic potential VV symmetric about x=1/2x=1/2, the answer to MathOverflow 283077 is yes. For real α\alpha set f(z)=y1(1,z)+αy2(1,z)f(z)=y_1(1,z)+\alpha y_2(1,z), with the initial data in the question. Then f′(z)≠0f'(z)\ne0 whenever |f(z)|<1|f(z)|<1. The prime here means differentiation in the spectral parameter. This conclusion holds for complex as well as real spectral parameters when VV is real. The original question has the real-analysis and quantum-mechanics tags and explicitly specifies real α\alpha, but does not specify the codomain of VV. The real-potential assumption is essential. A smooth complex even potential gives a counterexample below.
