The polygon bound for finite-spectrum quantum transport costs

26 November 2026

Abstract

Suppose the energy levels of an observable are ordered and transporting between two levels costs their separation raised to a power greater than one. We determine the smallest uniform error incurred when this cost is replaced by lower-exponent quantum transport costs, for every lower exponent in (0,1](0,1]. A single nondecreasing relabeling of the levels achieves the optimum. This proves the convex-power case of Iain Pitchford’s Conjecture 7.7. More generally, the scalar formula gives the entrywise uniform distance from a nonnegative hollow symmetric Monge matrix to the cone of ordered line distances. The proof combines a difference-constraints criterion with a counting argument for directed cycles. Original problem, Proposition 7.5 and Conjecture 7.7

Scope and priority

For 0<q<10<q<1, the polygon bound is zero by subadditivity. To see attainment in the cone directly, use the indicator Hu,t(x)H_{u,t}(x) of [u,u+t][u,u+t].
