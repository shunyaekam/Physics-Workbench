Abstract

We prove Gagatsos’s conjectured order-one-half Wigner–Husimi inequality for every state of one optical mode supported on zero and one photon, including arbitrary coherences and mixed states. A common noncentral Gaussian radius represents both sides for a pure state. The large-displacement argument uses the deficit in Gaussian Poincaré, and a finite rational coefficient certificate handles small displacement.

Theorem 1. Let ρ\rho be supported on span⁡{|0⟩,|1⟩}\operatorname{span}\{|0\rangle,|1\rangle\}. With [x,p]=i[x,p]=i and Q=⟨α|ρ|α⟩/(2π)Q=\langle\alpha|\rho|\alpha\rangle/(2\pi), α=(x+ip)/2\alpha=(x+ip)/\sqrt2, one has ∫|W|dxdp≤H(ρ):=18π(∫Qdxdp)2.\int |W|\,dx\,dp\ \le\ H(\rho):=\frac{1}{8\pi}\left(\int\sqrt Q\,dx\,dp\right)^2 . Equality holds exactly for vacuum.

This is Conjecture 1 of Gagatsos [1] on the optical single-rail qubit. The Wigner negativity of this subspace has been studied analytically [2]. Its pure states include coherent superpositions with a negative Wigner region, even arbitrarily close to vacuum.

Mixed states and equality.

In a pure-state decomposition the Wigner norm is convex and HH concave by the integral triangle inequality on the vector of square-root Husimi densities weighted by square-root probabilities [1]. Every nonvacuum state has a nonvacuum pure component with positive weight. The bound is strict there, while vacuum gives ∥W∥1=H=1\|W\|_1=H=1. This finishes the proof. A common displacement translates both integrals, giving the same result for every displacement of this class.
