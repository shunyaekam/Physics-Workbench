Can six photons share three perfectly matched colors?

In this pair-source model, the answer is no. Every higher-dimensional solution would restrict to three colors. Lean checks the complete six-photon theorem, including sources with arbitrary complex edge weights.

Lean checks the six-vertex theorem with arbitrary complex weights. The physics restriction was known; the rigidity proof is an attributed port of Rishi Rajasekaran’s work.

Quantum optics

Six photons and three target colors

Let the weight of an edge joining vertices i and j in colour c be w(c,ij). For monochromatic-edge graphs these 45 complex coefficients describe every matching amplitude on six vertices in three colours. Write F(c) for the sum of the 15 monochromatic perfect-matching weights. Normalization gives F(c)=1. Write M(c,e) for the sum of the three two-edge matchings of the four vertices complementary to the edge e.

For a fixed colour c define t(c,e)=w(c,e)M(c,e). At every vertex v, the sum of t(c,e) over the five incident edges equals 1, by expanding F(c) at v. If t(c,e) is nonzero, the edge is private to c among the nonzero weights. Indeed, for d different from c the colouring that has colour d on the endpoints of e and c elsewhere has amplitude w(d,e)M(c,e)=0. Thus w(d,e)=0. Further, F(c)=1 requires at least one perfect matching of nonzero colour-c weights. There can be no perfect matching containing one nonzero edge of each colour: the 2+2+2 vertex colouring induced by those edges has just this one contributing matching, whose product is nonzero.

The six-by-fifteen unsigned incidence matrix has the all-ones vector in the span of columns for which t(c,e) is nonzero. Choose an inclusion-minimal such set B(c): it has at most six edges by linear independence. Every bipartite component of its graph has equally large sides, since summing vertex equations over the two sides gives the same edge sum. This holds over the complex numbers, including for negative or non-real coefficients. Conversely the only left-null vectors of the unsigned incidence matrix on a connected graph are constant with opposite signs on opposite sides if the graph is bipartite, and zero otherwise; hence the component test is exact (over characteristic zero).

Relabel colours freely and enumerate triples of disjoint minimal supports B(c). Discard those already containing a rainbow matching. For each remaining triple extend each B(c) by one chosen monochromatic perfect matching avoiding the two other private supports, as every actual solution would allow. Every such extension contains a rainbow matching. The standard library enumerator computes 15, 90 and 70 minimal supports with 3, 5 and 6 edges respectively, 60 remaining unordered triples, and 960 extensions; none survives. Thus H1 holds. The standalone verifier uses exact integers, bit masks and exhaustive enumeration. It is a conventional finite proof, separate from the Lean verdict on the reduction. This restricted theorem was already known: A. Cervera-Lierta, M. Krenn, A. Aspuru-Guzik, Quantum 6, 836 (2022), https://doi.org/10.22331/q-2022-10-13-836.

A second enumeration chooses one nonzero perfect matching per colour. There are 3,375 ordered triples of choices and eight orbits under vertex and colour permutations. For each colour, every vector x of vertex labels in {−1,0,1} with positive sum gives a constraint: at least one edge ij with xᵢ+xⱼ ≠ 0 must have nonzero t. This follows by multiplying the six normalized incidence equations by the labels and summing. Keeping the minimal such sets of edges suffices. Applying these constraints, private-edge implications and forbidden rainbow matchings eliminates seven orbits by unit propagation. In the eighth orbit all three chosen matchings share an edge. Its complementary two-edge amplitude in red must vanish, since both endpoints can be coloured blue. Red normalization therefore requires a nonzero red matching avoiding that edge. Complete six-photon theorem

The exact catalogue declaration now has an end-to-end Lean replay. The runner builds the two public proof files, applies the merged reduction and checks the axiom set of the resulting parent theorem. The only reported axioms are propext, Classical.choice and Quot.sound. In the pair-source perfect-matching model on six vertices, no assignment of complex amplitudes realizes a GHZ state in local dimension three or higher.

The finite monochromatic contradiction is our formalization of a restriction previously established in physics. The rigidity step is an attributed port of Rishi Rajasekaran’s existing proof, including his upstream graph adapter. The contribution is a checked proof of the catalogue target.
