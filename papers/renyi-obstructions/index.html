Abstract

For one optical mode, consider the proposed bound of Wigner logarithmic negativity by the excess Husimi Rényi entropy above vacuum. A single photon violates it at every positive order below a*=0.288703…a_*=0.288703\ldots. At every fixed order above one half, including Shannon and min-entropy, a sequence of pure states violates it while its mean photon number tends to zero. This proves the dilute-superposition prediction of Gagatsos and raises the lower exclusion from the heuristic large-number-state estimate of approximately 0.170.17. The possible interval left by these obstructions is [a*,1/2][a_*,1/2]. We prove the bound throughout that interval on the full zero–one-photon Bloch ball and on photon-number mixtures through 144 photons.

Use the complex coherent amplitude zz as phase-space coordinate, with normalized densities q(z)=⟨z|ρ|z⟩/πq(z)=\langle z|\rho|z\rangle/\pi and WW in d2zd^2z. Entropy differences eliminate the constant coordinate Jacobian. Gagatsos asks at which orders log⁡∥W∥1≤Δa\log\|W\|_1\le\Delta_a holds for every state [1]. Equation (19) and Sec. VI of [1] already exhibit violations at orders 1,2,∞1,2,\infty and identify dilute-cat interference as the mechanism. Section VII predicts failure at every fixed order above one half as the separation grows. Theorem 2 proves that prediction with a single family whose energy also tends to zero.

Remaining issue.

The single-photon threshold specifies exactly where that witness stops violating the bound. Establishing the inequality, or finding a different violating state, for orders in [a*,1/2][a_*,1/2] remains a separate problem. The construction for high orders uses a diverging separation with vanishing occupation and supplies no violation at order one half.
