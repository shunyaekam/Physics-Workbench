Abstract

Twelve data qubits can encode four logical qubits at distance four using checks of weight at most seven. We exhibit a stabilizer code attaining seven, improving the upper bound eight in Table 2 of Wei et al. With three logical qubits the optimum is six, and with one logical qubit at lengths ten through twelve it is four. A graph-state census shows that every [[10,2,d≥4]][[10,2,d\ge4]] stabilizer has the same weight enumerator and requires generators of weight at least six. The twelve-qubit bounds follow from explicit Pauli generators and rational weight-enumerator certificates.

Completeness of the reduction. Extend any rank-eight stabilizer SS to a maximal isotropic subspace LL of the binary Pauli space. Up to the operations above, LL is the stabilizer of a graph state with symmetric zero-diagonal adjacency matrix Γ\Gamma. Optimality and source comparison

Wei et al. report [7,8][7,8] for Wopt(12,4,4)W_{\mathrm{opt}}(12,4,4), [4,6][4,6] for Wopt(n,1,4)W_{\mathrm{opt}}(n,1,4) at n=10,11,12n=10,11,12, and [6,8][6,8] for Wopt(12,3,4)W_{\mathrm{opt}}(12,3,4). Their Theorem 12 shows that any nontrivial stabilizer code generated at weight at most three has distance at most two. This analytic result supplies the lower bound four in all three one-logical-qubit cases.

Append one qubit in |0⟩|0\rangle to the ten-qubit certificate. Extend all old checks by identity and add ZZ on the new qubit. A Pauli commuting with the added ZZ acts by II or ZZ there. Multiplying by the new check therefore removes its action on that qubit, preserving its logical class and weakly decreasing its weight. Every old logical has a representative of the same weight on the enlarged code. The distance stays four, and the maximum check weight stays four. Iterating gives Wopt(10,1,4)=Wopt(11,1,4)=Wopt(12,1,4)=4.W_{\mathrm{opt}}(10,1,4)=W_{\mathrm{opt}}(11,1,4)=W_{\mathrm{opt}}(12,1,4)=4. For the two twelve-qubit cells, Wei et al. report the lower bounds seven and six, respectively, using their weight-enumerator linear-programming procedure (Appendix L, Algorithm 1). The rational identities above give direct checks of both bounds using the enumerator conditions of their Appendix G. The constructions attain both lower bounds.

Attribution of the subcode construction. Wei et al., Appendix J, prescribe appending a weight-four logical Pauli to the very same [[12,4,4]][[12,4,4]] parent to obtain their [[12,3,4]][[12,3,4]] code. We minimize the maximum generator weight across choices of logical Pauli and across generating bases. Of the parent’s 75 nonzero logical cosets with a weight-four representative, 60 give maximum weight six after basis minimization and 15 give eight. In the site order obtained by reversing Grassl’s printed matrix, appending XXIIIIZZIIII gives one of the 60. Likewise appending IZXIZZZIZI to Grassl’s [[10,2,4]][[10,2,4]] group yields a [[10,1,4]][[10,1,4]] code with maximum weight four. These are alternatives to the second and third columns on page one.

The first column instead preserves four encoded qubits by changing one stabilizer constraint of the parent. The parent admits an all-even stabilizer group, whereas the first column contains odd-weight elements. Qubit permutations and local Clifford gates preserve weight, so these two groups are inequivalent under those operations. Jing and Tu updated the distance-three entries of the source table and identified distance four as further work. Our comparison establishes improvements to the cited bounds. Historical priority for the values and code classes remains undetermined.
