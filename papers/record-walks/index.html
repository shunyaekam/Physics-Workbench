Every optimal record walk longer than one step closes.

At the smallest radius that fits a record-length walk, closing the loop is the only way to attain that length. A theorem of Reay and Zamfirescu covers large disks except for one 13-site star. A smaller explicit 12-cycle handles that exception.

Written proof for every record length. Lean checks the finite geometric witnesses. The general argument invokes the published Hamiltonian-cycle theorem.

Statistical mechanics

Why every record walk closes

Let rmr_m be the least enclosing radius of a self-avoiding walk of mm unit edges, allowing simple cycles. The triangular lattice has six neighbors at each site. A346126 records the lengths mm for which rm<rm+1r_m<r_{m+1}. If m≥2m\ge2 is a record length, every walk of length mm attaining rmr_m is a cycle. This uses both criteria in Pfoertner and Sigg’s problem: maximal length at that radius and minimal radius for that length. OEIS A346126 · Author’s illustrations

The disk graph. Let GG be the unit-neighbor graph of all triangular-lattice sites in a closed disk of radius R≥1R\ge1. Each grid line cuts the disk in an interval, so GG is linearly convex. To see connectivity, move from any site at distance d>1/3d>1/\sqrt3 from the disk center in a lattice direction within 30∘30^\circ of the direction to the center. The new squared distance is at most d2+1−3d<d2d^2+1-\sqrt3 d<d^2. The moves stay in the disk and terminate because it has finitely many sites. All sites at distance at most 1/31/\sqrt3 are mutually adjacent when distinct, since their separation is at most 2/3<32/\sqrt3<\sqrt3 and the next lattice distance after one is 3\sqrt3.

At a site of positive center-distance dd, the directions of its neighbors inside the disk satisfy cos⁡θ≥d2+1−R22d. \cos\theta\ge\frac{d^2+1-R^2}{2d}. Here θ\theta is measured towards the center. For 0<d≤R0<d\le R the right side increases with dd and is at most 1/(2R)≤1/21/(2R)\le1/2. Thus the selected directions form either the whole hexagon or a single circular interval at least 120∘120^\circ wide. There are at least two directions, and successive selected neighbors are adjacent. A site at the center has all six neighbors. Each vertex therefore has a connected neighborhood. A cut vertex would have neighbors in two different components of its deletion, which is impossible. Thus GG is 2-connected.

Reay and Zamfirescu proved that every finite 2-connected linearly convex triangular grid graph is Hamiltonian except the 13-vertex Star of David DD (their Fig. 4). Original theorem, 2000

Apply the theorem. Suppose an optimal record walk is open. It uses m+1m+1 distinct vertices in its minimizing disk. If R≥1R\ge1 and GG is Hamiltonian, the Hamiltonian cycle has at least m+1m+1 edges. Taking its first m+1m+1 edges (the whole cycle when equality holds) gives a permissible walk in that disk, against the record condition.

If G=DG=D, an open walk has at most twelve edges. The embedded exception has a center with all six neighbors and the six outer triangle tips, at distance 3\sqrt3 from that center. Opposite tips force R≥3R\ge\sqrt3. These coordinates also follow from the graph: the six neighbors of the degree-six center form a wheel, and for each consecutive pair their second common neighbor is the corresponding tip. For an alternative disk, use axial lattice coordinates with squared norm Q(x,y)=x2+xy+y2Q(x,y)=x^2+xy+y^2. The following cycle closes from the last point back to the first: (−1,0),(−1,1),(−1,2),(0,1),(0,2),(1,1),(1,0),(2,0),(2,−1),(1,−1),(0,−1),(0,0). (-1,0),(-1,1),(-1,2),(0,1),(0,2),(1,1),(1,0),(2,0),(2,-1),(1,-1),(0,-1),(0,0). Every edge has squared length one and every vertex lies within squared radius 7/37/3 of (1/3,1/3)(1/3,1/3). Its prefixes give a walk of every positive length up to twelve. This contradicts the minimum-radius condition since 7/3<37/3<3.

Small disks. If R<1R<1, two distinct lattice sites in the same parity class modulo 22 cannot both fit, because their separation is at least two. There are four parity classes, so an open walk has at most three edges. At m=2m=2, its three distinct vertices have mutual distances at least one. Writing p‾\bar p for their centroid and oo for the disk center gives 3≤∑i<j|pi−pj|2=3∑i|pi−p‾|2≤3∑i|pi−o|2≤9R2. 3\le\sum_{i<j}|p_i-p_j|^2=3\sum_i|p_i-\bar p|^2\le3\sum_i|p_i-o|^2\le9R^2 . A unit equilateral triangle is a three-edge cycle of radius 1/3≤R1/\sqrt3\le R, violating the record condition. At m=3m=3 an open walk has four vertices. They cannot all be pairwise at unit distance in the plane, so some pair is at least 3\sqrt3 apart. But the unit triangular cycle attains length three at the strictly smaller radius 1/3<3/21/\sqrt3<\sqrt3/2, violating minimality. This exhausts the cases.

This argument applies a published Hamiltonicity theorem. A bounded search for this exact corollary found the OEIS conjecture and the authors’ examples; precedence for the specific application remains unsettled. The derivation does not compute the entire sequence of record radii.
