# Second mathematical review of the finite-spectrum polygon paper

**Verdict.** I found no counterexample or invalid proof step. The manuscript gives a credible hand proof of the advertised finite-spectrum scalar and quantum assertions. I recommend revising the related-work and significance discussion before a novelty claim or journal submission. The same AI agent conducted the first and second mathematical reviews. This second pass used additional tests and sources. The manuscript and priority audit subsequently added the Monge attribution requested below. [First review](transport-review.md)

## Findings by severity

**Major for attribution, no identified effect on correctness.** The manuscript's interval atoms and its space of output matrices have a precise established name and description. A nonnegative hollow symmetric Monge matrix has a unique expansion in the symmetric corner-rectangle generators. The generator indexed by the paper's interval $[a,b]$ is Erickson–Kretschmann's $[a\times(n-b+1)]$, up to their harmless normalization. The target ordered line distance matrices are exactly the cone over their metric face (allowing pseudometrics on its boundary). Their Proposition 4.1 and §4.3 thus identify both the source cone of the scalar theorem and its target face. They do not give the sup-norm distance or prove that a nearest metric among arbitrary metrics can always be chosen on that face. State the scalar contribution as the Chebyshev distance to that face, including a realizing point and a chain certificate. Cite the earlier cone work. A literature search under the signed global-Monge condition alone misses this connection. [Erickson and Kretschmann, §§4.1, 4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.07522v2#S4) · [Rudolf and Woeginger, 1995](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01415751)

**Moderate for significance.** Pitchford genuinely poses the question in Conjecture 7.7, dated 15 November 2026, as the first follow-up priority in an explicitly unrefereed, AI-assisted release. Proposition 7.5 already gives the chain lower bound against all quantum costs and uniform pure-state error. Single-observable finite-spectrum power costs form a structured subclass. The new content is optimal ordered-line attainment in arbitrary size and hence the upper bound. No physical experiment, quantum dynamics, arbitrary multi-observable target, arbitrary exponent $p>1$, or new solution of Bunth et al.'s earlier inclusion problem follows. The source's printed conjecture omits $q>1$. The paper verifies the distance clause in the omitted snowflake range and refutes its line-attainment clause for three or more levels. “Settles/classifies the two clauses” is accurate; “proves the entire conjecture as stated” is false. [Actual conjecture and operator bound](https://github.com/ipitchford/quantum-transport-cost-cones/blob/270f5dba795ffd73d271bfde959237b730227c5e/manuscript.tex#L521-L563) · [Source's evidence and limitations](https://www.evidencepress.org/releases/quantum-transport-cost-cones/)

**Moderate for novelty calibration.** The closest-metric relaxation is considerably easier. Give every pair upper edge cost $d_{ij}+e$ and take the shortest-path closure. The closure stays above $d_{ij}-e$ exactly when every endpoint-minus-path deficit is bounded by $(\#\text{vertices})e$. For these monotone-in-interval weights, a path's local peak or trough may be deleted without increasing its cost, reducing it to an increasing path between its endpoints. This explains the polygon number for unconstrained metric fitting without the paper's counting lemma. Ordinary closure need not produce an ordered line: on levels $(0,1,2,100)$ at $q=2$ one has $e=392/3$. The three small-pair closure costs are $395/3,395/3,404/3$, with a strict triangle. The new result still has work to do in obtaining a single line, as required in the question. Existing Chebyshev consistency theory supplies the maximum over all directed cycles; it does not by itself restrict to one return edge and one increasing chain. The hollow Monge cone should now be a priority search term as well. I did not locate an earlier theorem proving this last minimax identity. Absence in this bounded search is inconclusive. [Classical all-cycle formulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/1802.01989) · [Metric nearness](https://doi.org/10.1137/060653391)

**Low for exposition.** The proof would benefit from a drawing of a maximizing interval and the two sorts of charged traversals. Explicitly specify that a traversal is the segment between consecutive surviving vertices after deletion of all strict-interior vertices, and that zero-sided/empty cases have coefficient zero. Merging duplicate colors at one index and using two-vertex chains with zero deficit are correct. The paper already distinguishes the partial Lean verification from the global hand proof. The cubic algorithm counts arithmetic operations; arbitrary real powers need an evaluation model for exact computation, as the rational/integer implementation recognizes.

## Attempts to break the proof

1. I rederived the sign of a cycle's atomic coefficient and tested the strongest step, the bound for disjoint *strict interiors*. A direct downward span of the maximizing interval cannot share a cycle endpoint with another such span. On an all-marked upward traversal, the entry arrives from a lower vertex and therefore cannot enter the head of a descending span. Its successor is the lower head; since it cannot exit the traversal to the right or back to the left, it too is internal. The entry is below this head and jumps to its upper tail, so it necessarily spans the maximizing interval upward. Disjoint internal vertex sets make the charge injective, even if atom intervals share boundary indices or have no cycle vertices on one side. I found no lost case from the cyclic starting point or an interval with demand greater than one.
2. The prefix recursion collects disjoint strict interiors, including the case of adjacent disjoint interiors with touching *closed* intervals. It bounds the total number of point copies, not just distinct points. In sorted order every strict interior contains a contiguous block of copies, which guarantees distinct colors up to the positive demand before merging at duplicate sites. Allowing demand zero and empty colors leaves the vertex accounting valid. The sign of the cycle total then follows by multiplying only by nonnegative atom weights. Distances from the super-source satisfy both lower and upper signed constraints. Taking prefix maxima genuinely restores monotonicity because enclosing interval costs are monotone.
3. For the quantum lower bound, the independent spectral measurements assigned to distinct indices in one chain are a joint construction for one cone summand. No physical joint measurement of noncommuting summands is needed. Pathwise snowflake triangle inequalities sum over the summands; each eigenvector product state is among the pure-state error tests. Weighted threshold cuts realize the same operator in $\mathcal C_p$, and product-state expectations are bounded above by the operator norm. Spectral degeneracy and infinite-dimensional multiplicity of finitely many eigenvalues leave these arguments intact.
4. The old tiny-model transcript rejects an unrelated proposal that treated the exponent as a spectral value. It did not read the cycle manuscript or challenge any of its premises. The previous mathematical objections about signed labels, omitted zero/empty cases, independent/noncommuting costs and the unrestricted $q<1$ quantifier are handled as above. The paper's numeric example attains $8/3$ with the printed labels. The existence of a negative result for $p>1$ does not contradict the stated regime.

## New falsification tests

The [replay script](transport_challenge.py) checked all 1,024 choices of binary weights for the ten nonadjacent six-site interval atoms; adjacent atoms add an exactly representable line potential. For each choice I independently enumerated all simple directed cycles and all increasing chains using exact rational scores, both with and without prescribed extreme endpoints. Their three maxima agreed. I separately solved 120 direct uniform-error linear programs with explicit monotonicity for integer-weighted Monge arrays up to 14 sites. Largest discrepancy against extreme-endpoint chains was $2.28\times10^{-13}$ (floating point). These tests include arrays unrelated to powers of spectral gaps. Replay from the repository root with `python3 checks/transport_challenge.py` using NumPy and SciPy. The six-site scores use integer arithmetic and exact rational comparisons. The LP comparison uses the floating-point HiGHS solver with an absolute discrepancy tolerance of $10^{-8}$.

A negative control makes the new assertion fail if one replaces the Monge hypothesis by plain interval monotonicity and superadditivity. On six sites numbered 0–5 assign length-one costs $(0,20,5,0,5)$, length-two costs $(20,25,5,5)$, length-three costs $(30,25,30)$, length-four costs $(50,55)$ and full cost $56$. These are nonnegative and superadditive under every split. The largest increasing-chain value is $25/3$; directed cycle $(0,3,5,1,4,0)$ has value $9$. Strictly positive off-diagonal costs can be obtained by adding $j-i$ to every forward cost, which preserves all deficits and signed cycle means. Thus a proof based solely on the triangle relaxation or monotonicity would have been insufficient.

**Residual uncertainty.** No external specialist review, no full machine proof of the global counting argument, and no exhaustive citation search establishing priority. The newly identified literature on the hollow Monge metric face warrants citation even if the attainment formula proves original.
