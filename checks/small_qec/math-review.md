# Independent mathematical review of the eleven-qubit optimum

25 November 2026 (Toronto). Reviewer: a separate oo research AI session, assigned after the candidate construction and proof were proposed. I checked the lower-bound argument from the commutation relations and reran the upper witness using direct signed Pauli multiplication, independently of the design search and its verifier.

**Claim reviewed.** Over all binary stabilizer codes on 11 qubits encoding five logical qubits and having distance three, the minimum achievable maximum generator weight equals seven. Weight counts nonidentity single-qubit factors. The search space includes non-CSS and degenerate stabilizer codes, arbitrary qubit connectivity and redundant generating sets. Any generating set contains an independent basis of no larger maximum weight. The lower bound applies to distance at least three. The exhibited code has distance exactly three. This matches the optimization in Definition 1 and the Table 2 entry in Wei et al. [Primary problem](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.19848v2#S7.T2)

## Adversarial checks on the lower bound

1. **Distance and basis changes.** Ignore Pauli phases when comparing syndromes. The syndrome of an error is the bit vector of anticommutations with a chosen independent check basis. Replacing that basis acts by an invertible binary matrix on every syndrome. Equality of syndromes, the zero syndrome and their multiplicities survive this change. Individual bit weights can change. The lower bound uses the new basis on both sides of its incidence count. If distance is at least three, every weight-one or weight-two Pauli with zero syndrome belongs to the stabilizer up to phase.

2. **Stripping one or two qubits.** A zero-syndrome weight-one error fixes a one-qubit Pauli eigenstate. Two weight-two stabilizers supported on the same pair and anticommuting locally on one site must anticommute locally on the other. Their common signed eigenspace on the pair is one-dimensional. Their local Pauli centralizer modulo phases is their two-dimensional binary span. For a fixed single-qubit Pauli the analogous centralizer is its one-dimensional span. In either case, multiply each generator by a factor stabilizer to clear its restriction to those sites. Its restriction to the other sites stays the same and its weight can only decrease. Factoring away the fixed subsystem therefore leaves a generating set of weight at most six, keeps five logical qubits, and preserves distance at least three: a commuting logical operator of smaller weight on the remainder would lift with identity on the removed subsystem. Repeating terminates with length n between 5 and 11 and binary stabilizer rank r = n − 5.

3. **Equal-syndrome classes and independent checks of weight two.** After stripping, all 3n single-site errors have nonzero syndrome and the three errors on each site have distinct syndromes. Two with equal syndromes multiply to a weight-two stabilizer. If distinct repeated classes use the same site i, choose one partner for each at sites j and k. Their pair stabilizers anticommute locally at i, so global commutation forces j = k and anticommutation on that partner site. This is precisely the removed two-site case. Thus repeated classes use mutually disjoint sets of sites. In each class choose one root and multiply its error by each other member to obtain a star of stabilizers. Every edge has a private leaf bearing a nonidentity Pauli, so all edges are linearly independent, also across classes. Their number t is exactly the number of extra occurrences beyond one representative per syndrome. One can extend them to a basis with r − t elements from the original restricted low-weight generating set by linear algebra.

4. **Syndrome budget including degeneracies.** A row of generator weight w has 2w ones across all single-site error syndromes: exactly two local Paulis anticommute at every supported site. Summing the new basis rows gives an upper bound of 4t + 12(r−t) = 12r − 8t. There are 3n − t distinct nonzero columns and t additional nonzero columns. For r ≤ 4, the former number is at least 2r + 15, exceeding the 2^r−1 available strings, including r=0. For r=5 and n=10, the 30 cheapest distinct nonzero strings have total Hamming weight 75. Deleting t of these can decrease their sum by at most 5t, and the t repeats cost at least t. The lower bound 75 − 4t exceeds 60 − 8t. For r=6 and n=11, the 33 cheapest cost 72. Their maximum weight is three. The corresponding lower bound 72 − 2t exceeds 72 − 8t whenever t>0.

5. **Pure equality case.** With r=6 and t=0 the budget forces the complete set of six weight-one syndromes and fifteen weight-two syndromes, plus twelve distinct weight-three syndromes. For each pair of rows i,j, the count of columns with s_i=s_j=1 is even. At a site with the same nonidentity Pauli it contributes two, at a site with different nonidentity Paulis it contributes one, and at all other sites zero. The count of the second kind is even because the rows commute. Summing over every row pair, the count must remain even. Counting instead by syndrome columns gives 15 + 3·12 = 51, a contradiction. This step needs no independence assumption about the choices of weight-three columns beyond equality of the budget.

## Independent upper-bound replay

I took the six unsigned generator words from the candidate file and assigned positive signs. The exact single-site multiplication table, including factors of i, yielded 64 distinct group elements, all commuting, and excluded −I. The common positive projector consequently has trace 2^11/64 = 32. Every generator has weight seven. Among Pauli errors of weight one or two none commutes with all generators. At weight three, exactly 79 commute and all 79 lie outside the stabilizer. One such logical operator is `XXIYIIIIIII`. A second computation obtained the same counts by averaging the coefficients of (1+3z)^(11−w)(1−z)^w over the full stabilizer group. Input SHA-256: `87db45ae65c642d6d01978ce918b3dbb693e6de2fab67ce0befc312e9657f640` (six LF-terminated generator words).

**Review decision.** The finite witness proves the upper bound and the syndrome argument excludes all smaller weights, including degenerate codes. The upper-bound replay is executable.
