# Prior art audit, 26 November 2026

**Verdict. The optimum was already publicly solved.** Yifan Jing and Yue Tu explicitly state (W_{\rm opt}(11,5,3)=7) in their September 25, 2026 manuscript. Their fixed commit contains a weight-seven construction, 37 unsatisfiability certificates for the lower bound, and an account of the reduction. The authors report Lean verification of their attaining construction, and DRAT verification of nonexistence for this parameter. Their full Lean nonexistence theorem concerns the different [[11,3,3]] problem. Their repo also states all six of the distance-three values proposed in our initial batch. The public pull request title names only [[11,3,3]]. Its body and pinned manuscript resolve the priority question. I inspected the source and file inventory without independently replaying the prior DRAT certificates. **Witness comparison.** I recovered the stabilizer from Steane's Eq. (29) by imposing the sign constraints of its five logical displacements and enumerated its entire unsigned group. One independent basis has weights 7,7,7,7,7,7. Enumerating physical phase-free Pauli strings in the normalizer outside the stabilizer at weights one through three gives (0,0,75) for Steane and (0,0,79) for our code. These counts are invariant under qubit permutations and local Clifford gates. This distinguishes our witness from Steane's. Jing and Tu's witness also has (0,0,79) and the same ordinary stabilizer weight enumerator as ours, so these counts alone cannot distinguish those two. No claim of priority for our construction or technique follows from these checks. [Steane, Eq. **What can be reported.** Our elementary parity contradiction could serve as a short alternative proof of a previously solved finite extremal problem. Jing and Tu use an exhaustive SAT reduction for this bound. Historical priority for the particular parity proof has not been established. A dashboard counting newly solved open questions should count zero for this result.
