# Independent mathematical review of the polygon-distance proof

**Verdict.** The proposed all-finite-spectrum proof establishes the operator-norm distance and attainment by an exponent-one function for $0<p\le1<q$. The same construction attains the bound for the source's uniform pure-state transport error. This is an independent AI mathematical review. External peer review and priority require separate evidence.

The versioned source gives a lower bound against the entire cone, including noncommuting approximants, for both notions of error. I checked the proposed argument in the other direction against this bound. For $p\le1$, the source's chain error is the positive part of its endpoint cost minus its segment costs, divided by the number of vertices. For $q>1$, take $d_{ij}=(x_j-x_i)^q$ for $i<j$ and let $\delta$ be the largest chain error, with zero included. [Pitchford, Proposition 7.5 and Conjecture 7.7](https://github.com/ipitchford/quantum-transport-cost-cones/blob/270f5dba795ffd73d271bfde959237b730227c5e/manuscript.tex#L521-L563)

## Steps checked

1. **Difference constraints.** On a directed edge from the smaller to the larger index the cap is $d_{ij}+\delta$. In reverse the cap is $-d_{ij}+\delta$. A simple directed cycle of length $k$ is feasible exactly when its aggregate backward cost minus aggregate forward cost is at most $k\delta$. Under these inequalities a zero-cost super-source and shortest-path labels give a signed feasible potential. If $z$ is feasible, its prefix maxima $y_i=\max_{h\le i}z_h$ still satisfy the signed pair bounds. For the lower bound take a maximizer $h\le i$ and use $d_{hj}\ge d_{ij}$. For the upper bound, any new maximizer $i<h\le j$ has $z_h\le z_i+d_{ih}+\delta\le y_i+d_{ij}+\delta$. Thus taking the absolute value in the physical pair cost is justified.
2. **Atomic expansion.** An interval atom $[a,b]$ contributes one to an edge cost precisely when that edge spans it. Two-dimensional finite differences of the convex power kernel give nonnegative weights. The adjacent atom has weight $d_{a,a+1}$. Summation telescopes to $d_{ij}$ also at the diagonal boundary $d_{ii}=0$. A chain from the outermost sites loses exactly the atoms whose *strict interiors* contain a chosen chain site. An endpoint equal to an atom boundary gives no loss. For a cycle the coefficient $h$ is the number of direct downward spans minus the number of direct upward spans. On an adjacent atom it is zero by flow balance across the cut.
3. **Packing inequality.** Take an atom $J$ with largest positive $h=r$, and write $b,f$ for the numbers of cycle edges spanning it downwards and upwards. Its $b$ descending edges have $2b$ distinct endpoints: tails are on the right, heads on the left, and each cycle vertex has only one predecessor and one successor. Mark these endpoints. For any other atom $I$, partition the vertices into the left and right sides and the strict interior. The number of left-to-right passages through the interior minus the number in reverse is exactly $h(I)$. This follows by deleting interior vertices in the cyclic word and counting left/right transitions; if one or both sides are absent, that coefficient is zero. In a collection of atoms with disjoint strict interiors, all internal vertices of these positive passages are disjoint. Each passage with an unmarked internal vertex consumes one distinct unmarked vertex. If all its internal vertices are marked, the first must be the tail of a descending edge spanning $J$, since its incoming edge starts to its left. The next vertex is therefore a head on the left side of $J$. This head is still interior to $I$, as the passage exits on the right and the head is below the preceding interior site. The entry vertex of the passage is left of the head. Its entry edge thus crosses the whole of $J$ upwards. Distinct all-marked passages have distinct entry edges. Consequently the sum of positive demands of any disjoint-interior packing is at most $k-2b+f\le k-2r$. Omitting nonpositive demands before applying this argument prevents cancellation from obscuring the deduction.
4. **Constructing the chains.** In the directed prefix graph each demand $h>0$ has an arc $a\to b-1$ with weight $h$, plus zero-weight steps forward between adjacent prefixes. A path collects only demands with disjoint interiors, and every disjoint family can be collected by such a path. Longest-prefix values define a multiset of interior sites by their successive increments, with at least $h$ copies across every demanded interior. Its total size is at most $k-2r$. Color the sorted copies cyclically with $r$ colors. The copies in any given interior are consecutive and thus use at least $h$ distinct colors when that atom demands $h$. One full-endpoint chain per color covers all positive atomic coefficients, while the sum of their lengths *in vertices* is at most $k$. If $r=0$ every coefficient is nonpositive and no chains are needed. A two-vertex chain has zero deficit. This gives the cycle inequality for any nonnegative choice of atom weights.
5. **Quantum realization.** Assign spectral value $y_i$ to the $i$th block of $f(A)$. Its exponent-one costs are $y_j-y_i$. These are sums of nonnegative successive increments across threshold cuts. Scaling the projector for a cut of weight $w$ by $w^{1/p}$ realizes that weight at exponent $p$, so the approximant lies in the full cone. Its block operator-norm error is the maximum absolute pair error. The source's lower bounds and this construction sandwich each claimed distance, irrespective of spectral multiplicities. With $q=1$ the original ordered labels already achieve zero. For $0<q<1$ the single-function assertion as printed can fail on three sites, as documented in the [source qualification](../discovery/transport-polygon.md).

## Independent computational challenge

[The verifier](transport_review.py) builds a different multicover from the proof's prefix longest paths. It greedily places missing copies at the latest strict interior site, then explicitly checks every reconstructed chain against every interval and the exact integer vertex budget. It exhausts directed simple cycles of all lengths at least two on the specified ordered set, including cycles which skip vertices. Run `python3 checks/transport_review.py --sites 9`. The argument above covers arbitrary finite size, independently of this finite test.

On nine sites the command checked 125,664 cycles, including 111,044 with positive demand. Maximum atom demand was three. Every coefficient and cycle budget passed exactly.

## Direct checks on the quantum lower bound and the remaining exponents

One can prove the required form of the source's lower bound without its retraction theorem. Select a unit vector in each spectral space of the target observable. For a second observable $B$ and distinct sites $i,j$, the expectation of $G_p(B)$ on $e_i\otimes\bar e_j$ equals $\mathbb E|X_i-X_j|^p$, where independent $X_i$ are spectral measurements of $B$ on $e_i$. For $p\le1$ the scalar function $|u-v|^p$ obeys the triangle inequality. Each chain therefore satisfies the polygon inequality pointwise, in expectation and after summation over the generators of an arbitrary cone cost. Operator norm error bounds each product-vector expectation error. It follows directly that each chain deficit divided by its vertex count is a lower bound.

For $0<q<1$ the distance is zero at every finite number of sites. In fact
$$h^q=q(1-q)\int_0^\infty\min(h,t)t^{q-2}\,dt.$$
For sliding intervals $[z,z+t]$, integration of the indicator that the interval separates two sites distance $h$ apart, with respect to $dz$, gives $2\min(h,t)$. The displayed identity writes the desired cost as a positive mixture of interval cuts. Group this mixture by subsets of the finite spectrum and omit the trivial cuts. Every remaining coefficient is finite since its cut separates a pair with finite total cost. As above, scaled spectral projections realize all the resulting cuts at exponent $p$. On any three distinct sites all triangle inequalities for the target costs are strict. Three distances on the real line always have one triangle equality. Thus single-line attainment fails for every spectrum with at least three distinct values in that exponent range. For one or two values a line representation always exists.
