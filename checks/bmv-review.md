## From the source criterion to the matrix inequality

Hägele uses two indexing conventions. The mean with $n$ copies of $A$ and $m$ copies of $B$ is $\mathcal A_{n,m}$, while $S_{p,m}$ indexes words by their *total* length $p$. Thus the mean here is $\operatorname{Tr}S_{15,3}/455$. Cyclically marking a $B$ reduces it to an average over 91 weak compositions of twelve into three gaps. All 455 linear words are represented with their multiplicities, including words with a cyclic symmetry. The comparator is the trace of the matrix cube $(A^4B)^3$. Hägele’s Lemma 2 with $A=e^H,B=e^K$ bounds this trace below by $\operatorname{Tr} e^{12H+3K}$. [Definitions and Lemmas 2–3](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00129v1#S2)

Section 4 assumes three-dimensional matrices, three $B$'s and arbitrary positive integer $n$. In an eigenbasis of $A$, the real part of the product $B_{12}B_{23}B_{31}$ can be negative. Pairing the other terms by their second index controls it by the three square roots in equation (6). I checked that its three terms give the two products in $Q$ and the product $XY$ when the eigenvalues are ordered as in the workbench. Lemma 6 uses $D>0$ when squaring the sufficient bound; in the complementary case the square-root comparison already holds. Its polynomial agrees with the workbench transcription. Replacing all deficits by $91$ times their values multiplies the polynomial by $91^6$. All six factors are homogeneous of degree twelve in the three eigenvalues. [Hägele, equations (3)–(6) and Lemmas 5–6](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00129v1#S4)

On the cone $a<b\le c$, divide by $b-a$ and put $x=a/(b-a)$ and $t=(c-b)/(b-a)$. These run independently over the nonnegative quadrant, including a zero smallest eigenvalue. Recovering the original eigenvalues multiplies the polynomial by $(b-a)^{72}$. Repeated eigenvalues follow by continuity; all equal eigenvalues give commuting matrices. The scalar certificate therefore covers singular and repeated spectra.

## Independent integer certificate and matrix controls

The [review checker](bmv_review.py) uses Python integers. It obtains the complete homogeneous polynomial of degree twelve by extracting a univariate generating-function coefficient. It computes a single signed Kronecker encoding of the sufficient polynomial at $x=B$, $t=B^{73}$. Since the total degree is at most 72, distinct monomials have distinct base positions. Replacing each subtraction in the generating expression by addition at $x=t=1$ gives an upper bound $M$ on the sum of absolute coefficients by repeated triangle and product inequalities. The base $B=2^{135}$ exceeds $2M$, so centered base-$B$ digits uniquely recover signed coefficients without any positivity assumption. The check recovers **2,324 nonzero coefficients, all positive, with minimum 2,912**. A deliberately negative digit and scalar evaluations outside the ordered quadrant test the decoder.

A separate matrix calculation evaluates the coefficient of $z^3$ in $\operatorname{Tr}(A+zB)^{15}$ by noncommutative polynomial multiplication. A real block embedding gives exact arithmetic on Gaussian integers. The 54 PSD pairs include 17 exact equalities, singular and repeated eigenvalues, and 14 prescribed negative-triangle cases, including non-real triangle products. For every one of the 42 diagonal-$A$ pairs, the raw word difference agrees exactly with the ordered index-path calculation after accounting for $455/91=5$. The other twelve pairs use full complex Gram matrices for both letters. On a strict test case the inequality reverses when $B$ is negated, and substituting the incorrectly grouped $A^{12}B^3$ for the balanced word fails. The coefficient calculation supplies the continuum proof. These matrix controls exercise the connection to the claimed inequality.
