# Which paper should oo research submit?

## First candidate · bounding the nonclassicality of light

Prepare one quantum-optics preprint around the question Gagatsos asks in Section VII. Which Rényi orders could let an intensity measurement in phase space bound Wigner negativity? The source proposes one half for all single-mode states and gives a heuristic lower cutoff near 0.17. The lab's single-photon example gives the boundary 0.2887033771… for that state. Every smaller positive order therefore fails as a universal bound. Distinguish this analytic obstruction from the extension proved on the zero–one-photon subspace, and from the conjecture for arbitrary light. Include the sparse-state certificate as a companion artifact if it helps the reader understand which cases survive. [Original question](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.30969v1#S7) · [Order manuscript](https://shunyaekam.github.io/oo-research/papers/renyi-obstructions/) · [Sparse states](https://shunyaekam.github.io/oo-research/papers/sparse-fock-coherence/)

As of 26 November 2026 the order certificate replay covered 18 strips and 10,467 terminal rectangles. A separate numerical evaluation reproduced the displayed single-photon root. Open editorial items are a specialist check of the all-orders argument and physical interpretation, a fresh search by theorem/formula and citations as well as title, and an accurate accounting of who derived, coded, audited and wrote each part. The present PDF's generic writing-assistance credit does not establish those facts. Treat priority as provisional.

## From result to submission

1. Write the experimental or theoretical consequence in one sentence. Freeze the exact statement and identify the closest earlier result. Search equivalent formulations, references, recent versions and independently implemented proofs. A catalogue tag starts this search. Record the search date and uncertainty.
2. Replay in a clean environment. Save the inputs, checker revision, command and expected output. Ask an independent reader to attack the bridge from laboratory observable to mathematical statement and the completeness of the search. File numerical leads as leads.
3. Trace the contribution to people and software. For a conventional paper Aryan Singh can use a byline where he made a significant contribution to that work, reviewed its reasoning and accepts responsibility for it. Constructing a test, choosing a meaningful physical observable or interpreting a failure may qualify. Directing the lab gives a project credit in other cases. Use an affiliation where this research was actually conducted. Describe the model, its role in discovery, verification code and writing, and the checks done by people. [APS authorship](https://journals.aps.org/authors/editorial-policies) · [APS AI requirements](https://journals.aps.org/authors/appropriate-use-ai-tools)
4. Once authors sign off, post the manuscript and immutable evidence together. arXiv quant-ph reaches quantum physicists; category endorsement may be needed. Consider peer review at Physical Review A for a substantive contribution to quantum optics. For AI-assisted process records, aiXiv accepts a link to an existing paper. It indexed Cinna's repository on November 24. Select any public evidence intentionally; raw tool transcripts need not be attached. A timestamp establishes when an artifact was deposited. ## What the lab should optimize

Use known, attributable solutions to measure whether the method can recover an answer, where it stalls, and what it costs. Mark whether the answer or an equivalent proof was available to a solver; public problems can be present in training. Use fresh physics targets for discovery and require a separate prior-art decision. In a comparison of solver policies, freeze the evaluator, assign both arms the same tasks and budget, count every failure, and change only the nominated part of the method. Reserve fresh tasks for evaluation to catch methods that improve only on the development set.

Track independently verified targets per fixed budget within each kind of problem, elapsed time to the first certificate, and token usage including failed trials. Show the number of unresolved or timed-out targets beside each success rate. Time waiting for review belongs in calendar latency; active job seconds and real model counters answer different questions. There is no defensible historical token-per-discovery figure for this lab.
