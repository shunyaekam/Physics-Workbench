# Priority review of the two-eigenvalue refined BMV comparison

**Disposition, November 25, 2026.** The three- and four-second-factor cases for a binary first spectrum have explicit earlier results for every positive first degree and arbitrary multiplicities. Our survey found no publication stating the same binary product comparison for *all* positive word counts, in particular at every second degree from five onward. The short all-degree deduction combines published theorems and ordinary coefficient positivity. Count it as a scoped corollary with provisional publication priority. It leaves the unrestricted parent conjecture open. [IQOQI Problem 40](https://oqp.iqoqi.oeaw.ac.at/refinement-of-the-bessis-moussa-villani-conjecture/) · [Hägele’s product comparison and reductions](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00129v1)

Here $A,B$ are complex positive semidefinite matrices in any finite dimension, $\operatorname{spec}A\subseteq\{a,b\}$ and the multiplicities of both eigenvalues are free. The claim bounds the normalized mean of all word traces with $n$ copies of $A$ and $m$ copies of $B$ below by $\operatorname{tr}[(A^{n/m}B)^m]$ for all positive integers $n,m$. The orientation matters. Swapping the letters preserves the word mean but can change the trace of fractional powers on its right. Hägele’s Lemma 2 also orders the two targets. At positive definite inputs, write $F(s)=\operatorname{tr}[(e^{s n\log A}e^{s m\log B})^{1/s}]$, nondecreasing in $s$. Our target is $F(1/m)$ and the mirror target is $F(1/n)$. Thus, when $n\le m$, a previously established mirror product bound also suffices, with singular inputs following by continuity. In particular the located unrestricted-dimension $(5,3)$ and $(5,4)$ comparisons cover $(3,5)$ and $(4,5)$ in this orientation; the small first degrees cannot be counted as new. ## Which part is a corollary?

Hägele’s symmetric path weight depends only on the counts of two possible eigenvalues. Grouping the spectral projections into the positive factors $C=\sqrt B P\sqrt B$ and $D=\sqrt B(I-P)\sqrt B$ expresses each grouped trace weight as a coefficient of $\operatorname{tr}(C+tD)^m$. Stahl’s theorem in the Lieb–Seiringer formulation supplies the positivity of all these coefficients. This explains why two spectral labels suffice for every $m$. Omega’s earlier three-label work already uses the positive spectral compressions. The combination with the ordinary *two-letter* theorem is the short deduction whose exact previous appearance we sought. [Classical coefficient formulation](https://arxiv.org/abs/math-ph/0210027) · [Stahl’s proof](https://arxiv.org/abs/1107.4875) · [Earlier spectral compression](https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/blob/c1938e29dc5ffac04e065367b179487fb4cd9744/Blueprint/D5/S3/Quantum/Analysis/RefinedBMVThreeSpectralValues.md)

There are two further direct all-degree overlaps. If $A=tP$ with $P$ an orthogonal projection, then the pinched and balanced products both equal $t^n\operatorname{tr}((PBP)^m)$, so the published all-degree pinching inequality alone covers the binary zero-eigenvalue endpoint. If either eigenspace of binary $A$ has dimension one, diagonalizing the compression of $B$ on the other eigenspace leaves a star of off-diagonal entries. Rephasing its leaves makes all these entries nonnegative, so Hägele’s Proposition 1 applies directly. This second observation is an implication of his criterion. The clearest scope beyond the earlier overlaps is two strictly positive spectral values, both multiplicities at least two, unrestricted $B$ and unrestricted second degree. [All-degree pinching](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.31851v1#S1) · [Path-weight criterion](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.00129v1#S3)

Dinh, Le and Vuong’s positive planar measure proves an all-degree inequality against $\operatorname{tr}(A^n E_A(B)^m)$, where $E_A$ deletes the off-diagonal blocks of $B$. Its right side can be strictly smaller than the balanced product even on binary spectra. For $A=\operatorname{diag}(1,2)$, $B$ the $2\times2$ all-ones matrix and $n=m=2$, the pinched comparison gives 5 and the balanced product is 9. Hillar, Johnson and Spitkovsky investigate eigenvalues of individual generalized words, a different comparison. [Pinching inequality, Equation 1](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.31851v1#S1) · [Generalized words](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0504573)
