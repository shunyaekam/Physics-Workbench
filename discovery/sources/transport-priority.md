# Priority audit of Pitchford's polygon conjecture

25 November 2026 (Toronto). Scope: approximating a single finite-spectrum observable's order-$q$ cost in operator norm by the order-$p$ cone for $0<p\le1<q$, and attainment of its chain lower bound by one order-one scalar function of the observable.

**Assessment.** The versioned author source leaves the arbitrary-number-of-levels case open. I found prior work establishing the general potential-fitting and cycle-mean machinery, but no paper proving that a maximum cycle mean for this signed convex power kernel always comes from one chord and an increasing chain, or that a single scalar observable attains Pitchford's value on every finite spectrum. If the new proof of this restricted cycle theorem passes independent review, report a proposed resolution of Pitchford 7.7 *in the convex-target regime*. Credit the author for the question, the lower bound and the three-level solution. This is a finite-spectrum mathematical-physics result about a single observable's transport cost. Neither publication priority nor mathematical correctness follows from this search. [Author's release](https://evidencepress.org/releases/quantum-transport-cost-cones/) · [Versioned paper](https://github.com/ipitchford/quantum-transport-cost-cones/blob/270f5dba795ffd73d271bfde959237b730227c5e/manuscript.tex#L522)

## Current source and nearby quantum results

I checked the upstream references, commit list, public issues and manuscript. The default branch and peeled release tag v0.1.0-candidate both point to `270f5dba795ffd73d271bfde959237b730227c5e`. The public commit list had one entry and the open-issue query returned none. Proposition 7.5 supplies the all-chain lower bound, Theorem 7.2 gives equality for three levels, and Proposition 7.6 gives an obstruction for some $p>1$. The conjecture, release and upstream priority file do not supply the sought upper bound. Bunth, Pitrik, Titkos and Virosztek's earlier Conjecture 9 asks about inclusion of the cost cones. That problem and its subsequent treatments concern representability in the direction of increasing order. Pitchford's reverse-direction best approximation adds an optimization and a one-function attainment claim. ## Closest preexisting mathematics

- **The classical source cone and its target face.** The unsigned spectral array is a nonnegative hollow symmetric Monge matrix. Its interval atoms are the symmetric corner rectangles in the classical Monge-cone decomposition. Erickson and Kretschmann identify the adjacent rectangles as the generators of the metric face of its normalized polytope. The cone over that face consists of ordered line distances with nonnegative gaps. These results establish the input representation and identify the target geometry. The present formula determines the entrywise uniform distance to that cone and constructs an attaining sequence of labels. The signed matrix used in the all-cycle potential problem is a different array. A targeted search on hollow symmetric Monge matrices, the metric face, Supnick matrices and additive line fitting found these geometric results but no proof of the chain formula. Priority remains unconfirmed. [Rudolf and Woeginger, original cone paper](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01415751) · [Erickson and Kretschmann, Proposition 4.1 and §4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2311.07522v2#S4)

- **Chebyshev consistency of pairwise comparisons.** Form the skew matrix $S_{ij}=\mathrm{sgn}(j-i)|x_j-x_i|^q$. Exponentiating gives a positive reciprocal matrix. Fitting $S_{ij}$ by additive scores $y_j-y_i$ is exactly the logarithmic Chebyshev approximation of this matrix by consistent ratios. The tropical spectral radius gives the maximum mean of *all* directed cycles after taking logarithms, and a potential achieves it. This reduction is established prior art. Karp gives a general cycle-mean algorithm. Neither result identifies which cycles are extremal for ordered power costs. [Krivulin and Sergeev, Section 3.3, Lemma 3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/1802.01989v2) · [Karp](https://www2.eecs.berkeley.edu/Pubs/TechRpts/1977/29121.html)
- **Uniform approximation by sums of one-coordinate functions.** Diliberto and Straus studied this approximation on rectangles. On a symmetric grid for a skew target, antisymmetrizing an additive fit produces a difference of scores with no larger error. Aliev and Guliyev obtain polygon-based formulas under a fixed mixed-difference sign on appropriate domains. This sign hypothesis fails globally here. For equally spaced levels $0,1,2$ and $q=2$, in the signed-power matrix the adjacent quadrangle using rows $0,1$ and columns $1,2$ has alternating sum $1+1-4-0=-2$. Its transpose has sum $+2$. Gavalec and Plávka require a globally Monge matrix for their cycle-mean result. A one-sided triangular array can retain a fixed sign, but independent row and column potentials on that triangle do not impose the shared score vector. I found no deduction of the required ordered cycle theorem in these works. [Diliberto and Straus](https://msp.org/pjm/1951/1-2/pjm-v1-n2-p04-p.pdf) · [Aliev and Guliyev](https://doi.org/10.59849/2218-6816.2026.2.195) · [Gavalec and Plávka](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0166-218X(02)00395-5)
- **Metric nearness and fitting a line.** Brickell et al. treat nearest metric matrices and shortest-path closure. The relaxation to arbitrary metrics can be analyzed directly: for nonnegative costs $c$, give every off-diagonal edge length $c_{ij}+e$ and take shortest paths. A feasible metric within $e$ exists exactly when the closure dominates every $c_{ij}-e$. The resulting path inequalities need not yield a line realization. Dhamdhere treats general metric data and approximation algorithms for line fitting. Here the input $|x_i-x_j|^q$ is generally nonmetric, the order is supplied, and the claimed optimum is exact. [Brickell et al.](https://doi.org/10.1137/060653391) · [Dhamdhere](https://research.google/pubs/approximating-additive-distortion-of-embeddings-into-line-metrics/)

For the classical signed fit, ordering its labels is elementary. Swap any inverted adjacent pair. Against a third label the two signed target values occur in increasing order, and matching smaller to smaller and larger to larger preserves the uniform tolerance. The swapped pair's own signed error decreases. Repetition yields ordered labels at the original error. The original research question is therefore concentrated in the one-chord cycle identity and the transfer of the matching scalar construction to the quantum cost.

## Search limitations

Searched the release title, author, conjecture number, source references, nearest metric and line fitting, additive distortion, logarithmic Chebyshev consistency, Monge and anti-Monge cycle means, signed convex displacement, and closed-polygon uniform approximation. Inspected the cited literature's results or abstracts and tested the relevant Monge sign condition explicitly. The query was limited to publicly accessible indexed material and the upstream repository, without author contact, a systematic citation-network review or searches of unpublished work. A resolution under different combinatorial terminology remains possible.
