How closely can a simpler cost match quantum transport?

  1. Start with energy levels. Imagine that moving between two levels costs the square of their energy gap. Can simpler costs reproduce every pair at once?
  2. Use a chain as a test. On levels 0, 1, 9 and 10, the direct jump costs 100. The three smaller jumps cost 1, 64 and 1. A simpler model obeying the triangle inequality has to absorb the difference of 34 across those four costs.
  3. Find the exact error. Relabeling the levels as 0, 9.5, 82 and 91.5 keeps every pair within 8.5 of its original cost. The chain shows that 34 ÷ 4 = 8.5 is the best possible. The paper proves that the worst chain gives the optimum for any finite spectrum and any target power above one.
  4. Check the result. A second AI agent reconstructed the proof. Lean checked the chain lower bound and one local step in the upper bound. The full argument is available for external review.

What did we prove about six qubits?

For every rank-seven pure state shared between two three-qubit registers, sorting the Schmidt coefficients in the computational basis minimizes nonlocal stabilizer Rényi entropy. Seven equal coefficients give log₂(343/211) bits. The paper also identifies three spectral regions valid on larger registers and proves an unbounded-rank result for equal registers with at most two exceptional amplitudes. Python and Lean check 210 inequalities for the original case. A separate exact calculation checks 630 inequalities for the three regions. Read the rank-seven paper.
