What did we prove about six qubits?

  1. Start with two labs. Each holds three qubits. Together they share a pure state with seven nonzero Schmidt coefficients. These seven numbers describe how the two sides are entangled.
  2. Ask what can change. Each lab may change its local basis. Researchers conjectured that sorting the coefficients in the ordinary binary basis already gives the smallest possible stabilizer Rényi entropy.
  3. Find a bound. Seven occupied directions leave one unused in each eight-dimensional register. That lets us derive an extra constraint on every basis change.
  4. Check the proof. An exact rational checker verifies 210 inequalities covering every choice of the seven coefficients. For seven equal coefficients the minimum is log₂(343/211) bits.
